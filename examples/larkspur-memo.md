# Investment Memo: Larkspur Robotics — Series A

**Prepared by:** Deal team, September 2026
**Ask:** $4M of a $12M round at $60M post

## Summary

Larkspur builds autonomous inventory-counting robots for mid-sized cold-storage warehouses. We recommend leading this round. The company has found a wedge in a market the large players ignore, the team has shipped hardware before, and early customer data suggests strong pull.

## Market

Cold storage is a $150B global logistics segment growing at roughly 13% a year, driven by grocery e-commerce and pharmaceutical distribution. Inventory counting in these facilities is done manually, in sub-zero conditions, with error rates our customers report at 4 to 8 percent. Mid-sized operators (50 to 400 facilities) represent about 60% of the footprint and are almost entirely unserved by warehouse automation vendors, who focus on the top twenty logistics companies. We estimate a serviceable market of $2.1B for counting and audit automation alone.

## Product

The Larkspur unit is a wheeled robot with a lidar and camera mast that drives aisles overnight and reconciles physical stock against the warehouse management system. It operates reliably at -25°C, which no competing robot we have evaluated does. Deployment takes under two days per facility and requires no changes to racking. The software layer, which flags discrepancies and generates audit reports, is where the company expects durable margin.

## Traction

Nine paying customers across 31 facilities, up from three customers a year ago. ARR is $1.4M with net revenue retention of 140% as customers expand to additional sites. The two largest customers each expanded from pilots to more than six facilities within four months. Average sales cycle is 11 weeks. Gross margin on the hardware-plus-subscription bundle is 38% today and the team projects 55% at scale as unit costs fall.

## Team

The CEO previously ran hardware operations at a drone logistics company through its acquisition. The CTO led perception engineering at an autonomous forklift startup for five years. The team of 22 includes six engineers who have shipped robots in industrial settings before. We consider this an unusually experienced hardware team for a Series A.

## Competition

Established warehouse robotics companies have not entered cold storage because their platforms fail below freezing and because the segment's operators are too small for their enterprise sales motion. Two seed-stage startups are attempting similar products; neither has a unit rated below -10°C. We believe Larkspur has a 12 to 18 month head start.

## Risks

Hardware margins may not reach projections if component costs stay elevated. Customer concentration is high: the top two customers are 45% of ARR. A large incumbent could build a cold-rated variant if the segment proves attractive.

## Recommendation

We recommend investing $4M. The combination of an ignored segment, a technically differentiated product, and a team that has shipped hardware before makes this one of the stronger Series A opportunities we have seen this year.
